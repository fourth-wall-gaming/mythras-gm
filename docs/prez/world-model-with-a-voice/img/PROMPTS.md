# Background images for the point slides

Eleven images, one per point slide. Drop each finished file into this directory
under the exact filename; the deck picks it up automatically (until then the
slide shows the filename in the top-right corner).

**Aspect ratio 16:9, at least 1920×1080.** Every image is used full-bleed with a
dark gradient over its lower third, and the sentence sits bottom-left — so keep
the lower-left quarter of each composition darker and free of detail, and put
the subject in the upper or right two-thirds.

## Shared style suffix

Append this to every prompt so the eleven read as one set. Change it here once if
you want a different look for all of them.

> Painterly oil on canvas, chiaroscuro lighting, a single warm lamp-amber light
> source against deep slate-blue shadow, muted palette, fine visible brushwork,
> cinematic widescreen composition, lower-left of frame in darkness, no text,
> no lettering, no watermark, no border.

## The prompts

### `01-title.jpg` — Title
A tabletop gamesmaster's screen seen from the player's side at night: a folding
screen, a spread of percentile dice, a hand-drawn map of a canal city weighted
down with a brass lantern, a half-full glass. Behind the screen, instead of a
person, a soft column of light. Through a window beyond, a Venetian-style city of
canals and water-stairs under a low moon.

### `03-new-medium.jpg` — Stories transport us. A role-playing game transports us as a participant
A reader in an armchair by a lamp, seen from behind, an open book in their
hands — and the room has dissolved at its edges into the story: the far wall is
a rain-wet canal street with lit windows, a boat passing, a figure in a hooded
cloak turning back to look at the reader as if waiting for an answer. The
threshold between the room and the street is soft, like a page turning.

### `06-dice.jpg` — Mythras: a complete, mature, realistic, and risky RPG system
Two ten-sided percentile dice have just landed on a dark oak table and show
double zero — a fumble — caught sharp in the lamplight. Around them, half in
shadow, the objects of every world the same rules have run: a bronze Babylonian
tablet, a Roman gladius, a Dark Age seax, a Byzantine icon, a rocket-ship
pulp cover, a well-thumbed rulebook with a cracked spine. A thin line of spilt
red ink runs from the inkwell toward the dice.

### `09-world-model.jpg` — A game is a simulation. A gamesmaster is a world model with a voice
A storyteller seen in profile, leaning over a table that holds a complete
miniature canal city — and the city is alive: real water moving in the
canals, tiny lit windows, boats under way, figures the size of grains of rice
going about their own business in the streets, none of them looking up. The
storyteller's mouth is open mid-sentence and their breath drifts over the
rooftops as weather. Only the storyteller and the city are lit; the room
around them is dark. The city fills the right two-thirds of the frame.

### `11-alhazen.jpg` — Memory is ontological
A medieval scholar's study in Cairo, a robed man in profile at a desk of
manuscripts, astrolabe and a brass camera obscura, one lamp. Above his head,
where the ceiling should be, the manuscripts continue as a constellation of
lit nodes joined by fine gold lines, like a knowledge graph drawn in stars.

### `15-night-canal.jpg` — The world moves whether or not you are looking
A narrow canal at night in a Venetian-style city. One upper window lit, its
shutter open a hand's width. Below it, wide rings spreading on black water from
something that has just gone in, and an empty boat drifting loose from a
water-stair. Nobody in frame. Distant lamps along the far quay.

### `19-the-ford.jpg` — Steer the world, never the character
A muddy river ford at grey morning. A queue of travellers — carters, a family
with a goat, three nuns, a troupe of tumblers — with a conspicuous empty circle
in it around two figures: a young woman with her hand in the ruff of a large
grey wolf, and a tiny ancient hooded woman leaning on a staff, looking at her.
Dragon-scale riders disappearing up the north road in the distance.

### `23-the-book.jpg` — Write it the way it was played
A freshly typeset hardback lying open on the gamesmaster's table beside the
percentile dice and the folded screen, lamp-lit, its pages dense with print.
Tucked into the gutter of the book, a torn scrap of handwritten notes in a
different hand — the player's own words.

### `25-infinite.jpg` — Text is the cheapest infinite there is
A single sheet of handwritten manuscript on a wooden table, lamp-lit. Where the
sheet's edges should end, the paper continues outward and downward into an
entire city — canals, bridges, towers, rooftops — drawn in ink and growing out
of the page as if the writing were becoming the place.

### `27-superhuman.jpg` — Superhuman gamesmastering
A gamesmaster's table seen from one end and receding into darkness, far longer
than any room could hold. Standing on it, in ranks that fade with distance, are
hundreds of small carved figures — knights, priests, thieves, boatmen, a woman
with a wolf — every one of them turned a different way, each attending to its
own business, none of them facing the viewer. Each figure casts its own separate
shadow from the single lamp at the near end, and the shadows all fall in
different directions, as though every figure has a light of its own. Beside the
lamp: one empty chair, a folded screen, an open ledger, and two percentile dice
still settling. Nobody is seated. The lamp is the brightest thing; the far end of
the table dissolves into unlit depth where the figures continue.

### `29-dawn.jpg` — Close: the moat is the curated ontology
The same canal city at first light, seen from a water-stair at water level.
Mist on the canal, the first shutters opening, a dredge-boat starting work. The
water is very clear and very still, and the reflection of the city in it is
drawn as a fine network of lit lines — the city, and its map, in one frame.

# CONSEQUENCES.md — consequence-driven campaign design

Agreed with the player on 2026-10-22. Apply after choosing a campaign pitch.
This supplements BRAINSTORMING.md and overrides forced-loss or fixed
sequence-count prescriptions in older planning guidance.

The complete post-selection workflow is
[SCENARIO.md](SCENARIO.md), added
after the Glass Hounds GM review. Use it to prepare cast, knowledge, adversarial
structure, discoveries, acts and playable situations; use this document for
executing their plans and resolving consequences.

## Design the future without the protagonist

Write a concrete baseline: what opponents attempt, what permits each step,
who suffers, and what the world becomes if nobody effectively intervenes.
Antagonists must be capable of winning. Inaction can cause serious, lasting
harm. Harm is neither a compulsory scene nor a tax charged for PC success.
Other people can resist too; include their resources and likely actions.

## Give each actor an executable plan

For every principal actor record goal, motive, assets and quantities, limits,
knowledge, next operation, fallback, surrender/bargaining conditions, and the
point at which their goals conflict with allies. Distinguish established facts,
intentions, estimates and unchosen branches. Never grant mind-reading or
replacement resources merely to keep an antagonist competitive.

## Connect motive to actual danger

Record what is valuable enough for someone to kill for if pushed, who would
choose that escalation, and under what circumstances. Then give that person
an achievable operation: hired searchers, an ambush, a capture attempt, a raid
or another action suited to the genre. Identify the intended victim, required
information, available force and stopping conditions. Money or a secret becomes
an adventure stake through what people will actually do for it.

Escalation follows motives, discoveries and surviving means. Do not kill a
witness to meet a quota, withhold official help by default, or force an injury
because the PCs have been effective. A beaten antagonist can surrender; cancel
their dependent hunts and sales. A credible threat that the PC prevents counts
as successful play, not a missing act.

## Build operations, not just clocks

Each operation requires: actor; objective; place; earliest/latest time; required
assets and information; warning signs; who can discover them and how; physical
opportunities to intervene; opposition; success/failure/partial outcomes; and
which later operations change or cease to be possible.

Check at each elapsed watch. Advance only when a concrete step is completed.
A deadline schedules an attempt, not automatic success. Resolve uncertain
offscreen opposition through the engine, once, and persist the result. Do not
advance time during OOC planning or count the same step twice. Cancel or revise
dependent operations after an asset loss. If actors replace an asset, record
the actual price, recruitment route and time required.

## Make danger legible and preventable

Provide multiple independent routes to crucial discoveries, including physical
evidence and people with reasons to speak. Obvious observations need no roll.
Failed checks must not erase every route to intervention. Show actionable
warning and enough time to respond before large irreversible consequences.
Secret plans may remain secret; do not make unknowable deadlines a punishment.
Keep explicit records of who knows what and how the news travelled.

## Organise three acts around changing opportunities

Act I establishes the threat and gives the protagonist a consequential first
objective. Act II exposes dependencies and competing plans, with several routes
to weaken them. Act III presents decisive opportunities created by earlier play.
Act transitions follow changes in the situation. Effective early victories can
shorten or replace an act; the ending is never owed a particular set piece.

For each act record: stakes, likely situation without intervention, available
player objectives, signs of escalation, turning-point conditions and possible
outcomes. Plan physical play, investigation, negotiation and recovery periods.

## Prepare outcome ranges

Describe clear victory, partial victory, negotiated settlement, failure and
withdrawal in concrete world terms. Track people, places and capacities saved
as carefully as losses. Outcomes follow achieved objectives and surviving
resources, not a GM assessment of whether the protagonist suffered enough.
Provide worthwhile rewards and let institutions and allies contribute.

## Stress-test and persist

Test refusal, disclosure to authority, early villain defeat, negotiation,
splitting enemy allies, paying debts and unusual player solutions. Repair plans
that require ignoring these options. Persist selected actors, operations,
clocks, knowledge and arc; retain spoilers in GM notes. Read the live records
back to verify. After each session reconcile plans with actual consequences,
update knowledge routes, and evaluate whether play was enjoyable and consequential.

Reusable operation template:

Actor / aim / assets / prerequisites / place / timing / warning routes /
intervention opportunities / opposition / no-intervention consequence /
success, partial and failure branches / downstream changes / knowledge holders.

# Style Card: Gully Burns (the author's own hand)

Derived from *... and then the Dragons came*, the 16-chapter Zorastor draft
(13,177 words, Oct 2023). That is a **different campaign** — Zorastor, Myrtis,
Lythande, Hakiem, Jubal, the Vulgar Unicorn. Take the voice, never the nouns.

## Sentence rhythm
Long accumulating openers that state the whole emotional situation up front
and then unpack it in clauses hung off commas — "shattered her utterly,
fragmented her so that it took her a year to emerge from a feral struggle of
daily survival to finally bury the dead, to speak the rites, and to lift her
eyes from the bloody ground." Then the paragraph shortens as it goes. Triads
everywhere: *stealing, begging, cajoling*; *raids, pogroms, and reprisals*;
*hardened, damned, murderers who thrived in slaughter and exulted in pain*.
Paragraphs run four to eight sentences and are broken by one-line paragraphs
used as drumbeats.

## Diction
Modern, warm, unembarrassed. Latinate where it earns weight (*sonorous*,
*deniability*, *incandescent*), Anglo-Saxon and profane where it earns speed.
Swearing is native to the narration as well as the dialogue — *fucking*,
*shitkickers*, *a nasty little shit*. Occupations and competence get named
precisely: a picker of locks, pockets, and misplaced trust.

## Dialogue
Loose tags, often none. In group scenes, deliberately anonymous — *"What now?"
Someone asked. "Justice." someone else said.* People interrupt, talk over each
other, and change the subject. Characters swear at each other affectionately.
Long speeches are allowed when a character is explaining the world; they are
not trimmed for realism.

## Description density
Bursts, not sweeps. One or two exact details and move on — a staff *burnished
for combat, not long walks by the sea*; an innkeeper's bow *conspicuously
missing his thumb*. Smell and noise before sight. Rooms get a sentence;
people get a paragraph.

## POV and tense
Close third, past tense, **one head per chapter**, named in the opening line.
Rotate freely between chapters — protagonists, allies, and at least once the
enemy. Free indirect discourse throughout: the narration slides into the
character's own words without quotation. *Fuck. Just the sort of thing you
don't need.*

## Signature moves
- **Small-caps lead-in.** Every chapter opens with its first clause in caps
  running straight into lowercase: `AS THE HOOD WAS RIPPED FROM HIS HEAD,
  RANDALL SUDDENLY realized where he was.`
- **"A beat."** Used bare, as its own paragraph, to mark timing. Also
  *Another beat.* / *There was a beat, and then her composure shattered.*
- **The aphoristic aside to the reader**, usually second person or general:
  *It is never wise to appear distracted on the open streets.*
- **The hard chapter button** — a short flat line that closes the chapter and
  often reverses it: *This was a mistake.* / *These were the catacombs, after
  all.* / *But even thieves had lines that could not be crossed.*
- **Comic undercut after intensity.** A goddess delivers a prophecy and then
  swears like a docker; a woman wakes from a holy vision and shouts *who are
  you even talking about?* at her wrecked bedroom.
- **Epithets** conferred in dialogue and then adopted by the narration — the
  Lakelady's Javelin, the woman from the woods, the pig woman.

## Never do
Never be cold. Never withhold interiority — this narrator tells you what
people feel and why, and is fond of them. No iceberg theory, no clipped
minimalism, no refusing the emotional payoff. Don't sanitise the sex work or
the violence, and don't leer at either. Never use the Zorastor nouns.

# Art-Style Card: First Through

Documentary photography of a place that exists. Not concept art, not space art,
not an illustration of an idea — a picture somebody took, on the job, because
they were standing there. Used as the preamble in front of every per-scene
prompt so the whole set reads as one body of work.

## Medium
Photographic realism, in the register of long-form reportage: a working
photojournalist with a fast prime lens in available light. 35mm or 50mm
equivalent, shallow-to-moderate depth of field, visible grain. Where a subject
cannot be photographed — a species reference, a station in cutaway — switch to
muted gouache technical illustration of the kind a field guide uses, never to
painted space art.

## Palette and value
Desaturated and cold by default: steel, worn aluminium, oxidised copper,
sodium-orange where there are working lamps. Two light sources in most frames
and they disagree — hard blue-white from a star or a ring housing, warm
low-colour-temperature from human fittings. Colour is allowed exactly one
loud exception per image, and it is always a living thing: a glint, a membrane,
lamp-fed greenery.

## Line and texture
Everything is used. Deck plate worn to a shine along the walking line, scuffs
where crates are dragged, paint worn off handrails at hand height, dust in the
corners of a room nobody cleans. Surfaces are scratched metal, moulded
composite and grown membrane — no brushed-chrome, no glass panels, no seamless
white.

## Composition
Mid-distance, eye level, nobody posing. Frame the work rather than the hero:
people are doing a job and the camera caught them at it. Honest wide shots for
places; waist-up for people. Let things be cut off by the frame. Negative space
is empty deck or black sky, never decorated.

## Subject canon
**The Nine** is a rotating cylinder two kilometres long: floors curve *upward*
away from the viewer, and the anchor hall at its waist has nine metal rings set
flush in the deck, each four metres across, with a queue at each. Sirius is a
hard blue-white point no filter quite tames; its white dwarf is a separate
smaller meaner point.

**A membrane** is a living second skin, close-fitting, translucent and faintly
iridescent, worn as ordinary clothing. It **clouds** like breathed-on glass
across the shoulders and forearms with use — that clouding is a person's
career, visible, and the most important detail on any first-through.

**Boots never match.** Nothing dead crosses a door, so everyone is wearing two
boots from two different stations.

**Everybody is young.** First-throughs are twelve to twenty-two. The adults in
a hall are staff, passengers and cargo.

**The architecture was built for five peoples**: ramps instead of stairs, a dim
corner left in every concourse, duct grilles cut fine enough for a glint to
pass and nothing larger.

## Mood
Competence under pressure, affectionately observed. Nobody is heroic and
everybody is good at their job. Quiet, unglamorous, warm toward its people and
cold about its physics.

## Never
No space opera. No sleek ships, no glowing holographic displays, no floating
translucent interfaces, no lens flare, no nebulae, no planets hanging
picturesquely in a viewport. No chrome. No helmets with visor glow. No weapons
drawn. No anthropomorphic aliens, no bug-eyes, no tentacles, no mascots, no
faces where a species has no face. Nothing is cute. Children are not plucky and
are never lit from below like a poster.

_Touch._ Fastens a portion of a spirit to a physical object. The object does not contain the spirit — it *holds a piece of it*, and thereafter every working performed on that spirit is easier for whoever holds the anchor.

Two kinds are traditional.

- **A primary anchor** is something that was the spirit in life: bones, most often. It is heavy, it cannot be replaced, and it is normally kept somewhere defensible and never moved.
- **A working anchor** is small and carried — a locket, a ring, a stone. It is the piece the daily rite runs through.

**Anchors are the whole vulnerability of the practice.** Take one and the rite gets harder. Take both and the binding begins to fail on its own. Destroy the primary and the spirit is loose.

Old ground where many died badly — a drowned district, a battlefield, a plague pit — is thick with material for anchors, which is why practitioners dig in such places and why doing it carelessly produces hollow men.

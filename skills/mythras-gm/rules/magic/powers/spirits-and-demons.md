The binding and the Wild Line act on spirits. This page says what a spirit
*is* at the table, what turns up when somebody untrained opens a channel, and
how strong a seat is when somebody tries to break it.

## Spirits by rank

A spirit has **POW**, **Willpower** and a few **manifestations**: things it can
do through a host or in a place. It has no hit points and cannot be hurt by
weapons.

| Rank | POW | Willpower | Manifestations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A Wanting** — nameless, one hunger, does not speak beyond it | 2d6+6 | POW×2 +20 | **One**, tied to its hunger. Seated, it gives the host one gift, and over months or years eats the host's self until what is left is madness |
| **A Named** — a Wanting that fed through many hosts and grew a name; speaks, bargains, lies, remembers | 3d6+9 | POW×2 +40 | **Two to four**, and a will of its own. **Knowing its name makes binding it one grade easier** |
| **A Crowned** — one of the greatest, with a following of lesser demons | — | — | **No statistics.** A Crowned is not an opponent in a roll; it is the subject of a campaign. Seated, it rules, and the host becomes its face |
| **The unquiet dead** | as in life | as in life | As a Wanting or a Named, by how long and how hard they have been held. **They have nowhere to be sent back to** |

**A typical manifestation** lasts one action: a **surge** (treat as STR 16 for
that action, a natural weapon of 1d3 or 1d4); a **presence** that forces a
Willpower roll to act against the host; a **heat** or a **cold** in a room; a
**voice**. Write one line for each.

**Demons and the dead differ when driven out.** A demon driven out goes back
where it came from, or is **loose in the room**. The dead have nowhere to go:
driven out of a host they are loose and still held by whatever holds them (their
bones, an anchor), and only ending *that* ends them.

**Damnation**, where a setting uses it: whoever dies with a demon seated in them
does not go to rest but into that demon's master.

## What answers an open channel

**Only a trained binder with an anchor gets what they called.** Anybody else —
an apprentice, a scroll-reader, a fool with a knife — opens the channel and gets
**whatever answers**. Roll d10, or choose where the scenario has fixed it:

| d10 | What answers |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | **A Wanting of appetite** — lust, hunger, greed. It will take the seat if the seating roll succeeds |
| 4–5 | **A Wanting of something else** — rage, terror, madness — and it will take the seat just as willingly |
| 6 | **Something unquiet**: a local dead thing, drawn by the blood |
| 7–8 | **Taint only.** Nothing seats; the wound festers, Spirit Sight shows residue on the host and the room, and the host carries a new minor Passion that is not theirs |
| 9–10 | **Nothing.** The channel stays open and visible to Spirit Sight, and does not heal |

A failed seating roll with anything from 1 to 6 leaves the thing **unseated and
loose**, and it does what its hunger wants with whoever is nearest.

## Scrolls of a power

A **scroll of one step** of the binding lets somebody who has the **core power**
perform that step **once, one grade harder**, as `magic/scrolls`. It is
consumed whatever the result. Seat the Bound from a scroll is therefore **Hard
Willpower** against the host's Willpower. Without the core power, a scroll of a
power is paper.

## A seat's Intensity

For anything that tests a seat or an anchor by Intensity or Magnitude — Exorcism,
Dispel Magic, Spiritshield — a seat has:

> **Intensity = the binder's POW ÷ 3 (round down), +1 for each anchor that holds it.**

A working anchor (a locket, a ring) has the same Intensity as the seat it holds.

## What breaks a seat, and what breaks an anchor

| | A seat (spirit in a host) | An anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Unseat** (Wild Line) | Yes: opposed Willpower; the spirit comes out loose | No |
| **Exorcism** (Cleric 3) | Yes, if the seat's Intensity is no more than half the spell's. A spirit that wins the duel may possess the loser | Yes: Exorcism also ends domination by objects, so it breaks a working anchor on the same test |
| **Dispel Magic** (Cleric 2, Mage 2) | Only a **weak seat**: one whose Intensity is less than the spell's. It does nothing to the spirit itself, which is **loose** once the seat goes | Yes, a **powerful** one: Intensity greater than the anchor's |
| **Draw Forth** (Binding) | Yes, by its own binder, into somewhere else | — |
| **Protection from Evil** (Cleric 1) | Prevents a new seat; no effect on one already made | — |
| **Spiritshield** (Cleric 0) | A spirit must beat the caster's Piety with its Willpower to enter | — |

The powers themselves — Spirit Sight, the binding's Boosts, the Wild Line — are
never Dispelled. What a powerful Dispel Magic or an Exorcism breaks is the
**working left behind**: the seat and the anchors.

**Breaking an anchor does not free the dead.** It frees them from the binder.
What holds a ghost in the world (its bones, its unfinished rite) is not a
working and no spell breaks it.

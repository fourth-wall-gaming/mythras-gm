**Read this before ruling on any casting.** Both books are loaded and they do not
agree, so landing on the wrong one produces a ruling that reads exactly like a
correct one.

| | Mythras Imperative (`magic/casting`) | Classic Fantasy Imperative (`magic/cfi/*`) |
|---|---|---|
| casting skill | Magic (POW+CHA) | **Channel (INT+CHA)** for divine, **Arcane Casting (INT+POW)** for arcane |
| intensity | every spell is Intensity 1 | **1 to skill/10**, chosen at casting; Magnitude is always the maximum |
| cost | 0 / 1 / 1 / 1d3 by success level | **per the spell's own Cost line**, halved on a critical, nothing on a failure |
| failure | 1 MP spent | **no MP spent** — or force it, paying in full and losing the spell from memory |
| fumble | 1d3 MP | **half cost, and the spell is expunged** |
| spell list | twenty-four (`magic/spell-list`) | a hundred and forty-three (`magic/cfi/spell-list`) |

**Classic Fantasy Imperative governs.** The Mythras Imperative magic rules are
kept because the rest of Mythras Imperative — combat, skills, the game system —
is the chassis underneath, and because the Veilwrack campaign in this same graph
does use them.

Where both books name the same spell — Light, Sleep, Calm, Chill, Darkness,
Heal, Knock — **they are not the same spell**, and the one in play is the one
under `magic/cfi/`.

## The one thing that is neither

**Piety.** Gardwen and Nerissa both cast on a Piety skill. It is not a Mythras
skill and it is not a Classic Fantasy Imperative skill — it appears once in the
whole CFI SRD, inside the text of Spiritshield, and is nowhere defined. It comes
from full Classic Fantasy.

Played as: **Piety is devotion, not casting.** It is the cleric's standing with
their god — what an Exhort is rolled against, what a rite is performed on, what
falls when they act against the faith. **Channel is what casts the spell.** A
cleric with high Piety and poor Channel is devout and clumsy, which is a real
kind of priest.

This is ours, written here because two character sheets depend on it and nothing
recorded what it meant.
